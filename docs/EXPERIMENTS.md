**How to read the lane numbers (M3.3–M3.4).** IoU = ego-lane mask vs BDD drivable area `direct` over image rows 0.58–0.74; single-side fallback on (the missing boundary is synthesized at 0.37 of the bird's-eye width), and 'measured-only' scores those images as no prediction. F1 = measured boundaries only vs the nearest BDD parallel lane lines, hit if mean error ≤ 20 px. Full table: `docs/figures/lanes_comparison.md`; failure cases: `docs/figures/lanes_failures.jpg`.
- **YOLOP finds the right lines far more often** (F1 0.52 vs 0.23, precision 0.60 vs 0.24). Classical is fooled by car edges, hood reflections and road text.
- **Ego-mask IoU is a tie (~0.42)** because YOLOP returns no line at all on 129/500 images (classical 28/500), mostly faint/worn markings, snow (21/38) and dense traffic. When it predicts, its IoU is higher (0.52 vs 0.45).
- **Night costs YOLOP little** (F1 0.49 vs 0.52 day). Snow is the worst condition for YOLOP (F1 0.37). Curves are weak for both (n = 20).
- **Speed:** classical 40 ms on CPU, YOLOP 61 ms end to end on T4 FP32. Two YOLOP model timings exist: 42.2 ms is `session.run` inside the eval loop (real 1280x720 frames, letterbox, host I/O); 22.9–24.9 ms is a fixed-tensor micro-benchmark (median of 50, with and without CUDA I/O binding). The M6 benchmark (`docs/benchmarks.md`) re-measured all backends with one harness: YOLOP PyTorch FP32 model time 22.78 ms.
- **Decision:** use YOLOP as the default lane backend for the live pipeline; keep classical as the CPU fallback and for the report comparison. BDD lane width from 279 images is 0.407 W at the band bottom (≈0.34 bird's-eye), slightly below the 0.37 used; leave as is (affects only synthesized sides).
- **Caveats for the report.** The BDD warp (`configs/lanes_bdd.yaml`) and the 0.37 fallback width were tuned on BDD val ground-truth statistics, and part of the 30-image tuning sample lies inside the 500 evaluated images; re-derive on the train split before any retune. About half of the ego masks use a synthesized side (classical 249/500, YOLOP 222/500), so the headline IoU depends on the fallback; the measured-only IoU is the conservative number. F1 counts a predicted line on an image with no scorable GT as a false positive (108 such images), which penalises classical more because it predicts more often.

**M4.4 notes (re-run of 2026-11-13, commit 5655462, W&B `amgl803i`; fusion in inverse depth).** GT is the median LiDAR depth inside the 2D box and the 3D label box (visible surface); scored on KITTI GT boxes so the numbers measure distance only, not detection. LiDAR projection was checked on smoke overlays (points land on poles, guardrails, cars). Overall AbsRel: geometric 0.168, fused MiDaS-small 0.170, MiDaS-small alone 0.236, DPT-Hybrid alone 0.234.
- **Fusion bias fixed.** The first run ([0zvulu0t](https://wandb.ai/anish-grd2004-vellore-institute-of-technology/aiml04-perception/runs/0zvulu0t), commit 61e8ac9) averaged in metres and pulled fused distances short: bias -0.032 overall, -0.078 at 30–60 m. Fusing in inverse depth gives bias -0.001 overall and -0.029 at 30–60 m, with AbsRel unchanged (0.168 -> 0.170) and RMSE 6.76 -> 7.05 m (far estimates are no longer shrunk). Geometric and MiDaS-only rows are identical between the runs.
- **Geometry carries the accuracy; MiDaS adds coverage and range.** The table rows cover different objects (fused estimates 0.99–1.00 of objects, geometric 0.81–0.96, because truncated/occluded boxes have no visible ground contact). On the common subset (objects every method estimated, `docs/figures/depth_comparison.md`): AbsRel geometric 0.169 vs fused MiDaS-small 0.165, RMSE 7.55 vs 6.67 m; at 30–60 m 0.234 vs 0.210; at 0–10 m 0.086 vs 0.099.
- **MiDaS-small is the live model.** DPT-Hybrid is no more accurate after alignment and costs 424 ms per frame at its KITTI input 384x1248 (2.4 FPS) vs 27 ms at 256x832 (37 FPS) for MiDaS-small on T4. At the live input 256x448, MiDaS-small's module time (with pre/post-processing) is 14.7 ms (FP32, PyTorch); the M6 benchmark's model-only time is 10.95 ms.
- **Caveats:** KITTI camera height/pitch are known exactly, which flatters the geometric method; CARLA and BDD values in `configs/camera.yaml` are placeholders until M5. Kalman/TTC is unit-tested only (KITTI object frames are not sequences); it gets evaluated on CARLA recordings in M5. TTC is reported only after a track is 0.75 s old and closing faster than 2 sigma of its speed estimate; simulated stationary cars at 10–20 m give no false TTC, rare ones remain at 30–40 m. Full table: `docs/figures/depth_comparison.md`.

"Pipeline FPS" is model/stage time only; end-to-end includes PNG decode, drawing and H.264 encode. 99.7 % of objects get a distance; QA recomputed every summary number from `frames.jsonl` and found no distance bias against flat-ground distances on untruncated boxes (median ratio 1.00–1.01 per bin).
- **False leads (v2).** With one lane line measured, the synthesized side spilled over the kerb and parked cars became the lead (frame 319: LEAD at 6.1 m). v3 added a metric lateral corridor (`fusion.ego_lane` in `configs/depth.yaml`); v4 kept it at all ranges with a curve allowance (`|X| <= 1.5 + Z^2/(2*400)` m with trusted lanes, 1.2 m up to 30 m without) and restarts the Kalman filter when a track is re-acquired with a jumped distance (coasted predictions had reached 1.2 m, TTC 0.12 s).
- **Remaining (v4).** The one AEB-trigger frame (224) is a car crossing ahead at 7.7 m, 1 cm inside the corridor for one frame. 47 of the 111 lead frames are on objects ≥ 2.2 m lateral at > ~24 m, where the curve allowance lets the lane mask decide; 4 of 5 slow-down (TTC 2–4 s) lead frames are of that kind. M5 `control_node` must require a lead condition to persist over several frames, and the long-range corridor needs tightening (lane-depth follow-up).

- **Sim-to-real gap, detection.** CARLA mAP@0.5 0.530 vs 0.554 for the same 6 classes on BDD val, so the overall number barely moves, but per class it does: car 0.694 (BDD 0.775), truck 0.481 (0.582), bicycle 0.307 (0.425), motorcycle 0.104 (0.383) drop; person 0.756 (0.587) and bus 0.840 (0.572) rise (CARLA pedestrians and buses are large, uncluttered and few: 314 and 78 boxes). Fine-tuning on BDD gains only +0.045 mAP@0.5 over COCO zero-shot here (+0.19 on BDD), so most of the BDD gain does not transfer to rendered images; this is the case for the M5.9 BDD + CARLA fine-tune. mAP@0.5:0.95 is depressed by the GT itself: CARLA boxes are projected 3D-box corners, looser than a tight 2D box (bicycle 0.083, person 0.265 at 0.5:0.95).
- **Weather.** Each weather is a different 500-frame route segment with a different class mix (bicycle/motorcycle appear in some blocks only), so the mixed-class per-weather mAP (0.35–0.69) is not comparable across weathers. Car AP50 is: ClearNoon 0.89, WetNoon 0.85, CloudyNoon 0.68, ClearSunset 0.59, ClearNight 0.52, HardRainNoon 0.41. Night and heavy rain cost ~0.4 AP50 on cars in CARLA, far more than on BDD (night −0.03); still confounded with the route segment.
- **Distance: the flat-ground geometry is right in CARLA.** On GT boxes geometric AbsRel is 0.040 with bias +0.008, and the camera height implied by the GT (Z·(v−cy)/fy, 2,789 untruncated boxes at 5–40 m) is 1.594 m median (p10–p90 1.49–1.64) vs 1.6 m configured. So the PLACEHOLDER height/pitch in `configs/camera.yaml carla` are confirmed by data.
- **Detected boxes read ~10 % long.** Detector boxes are tighter at the bottom than projected 3D boxes (detected/GT-box geometric distance median 1.11 at 0–10 m), so distances on detections are biased long: +0.07 at 10–30 m, +0.05 at 30–60 m. The 0–10 m mean (AbsRel 0.28–0.32) is dominated by cars alongside the ego at GT 0.4–1 m (the nearest 3D-box vertex sits almost beside the camera; the box bottom can't represent it); the median relative error at 0–10 m is +0.12. Read long means AEB/ACC acts slightly late, which matters for the 8 m distance rule; the closed-loop runs stopped with 6.5 m gaps.
- **MiDaS adds little in CARLA.** Aligned MiDaS-small alone is poor (AbsRel 0.36, coverage 0.46–0.57, because few frames have enough anchors) and fusing it makes GT-box AbsRel worse than geometric (0.087 vs 0.040). That is the opposite of KITTI, where geometric was noisier (0.168). Oracle-aligned MiDaS vs GT depth is AbsRel 0.245 (HardRain 0.351, night 0.186). Coverage is the only gain (fused 0.99 vs geometric 0.97 on GT boxes; truncated boxes). `fused_live` (anchors keep their own fit, as in the live node) is close to the leave-one-out `fused`.
- **Caveats.** The GT distance is the nearest bbox vertex depth, not the visible surface. Unlabeled objects (visible fraction < 0.25, > 80 m, < 6 px) count as false positives. No GT for riders, traffic lights or signs, so those detections are ignored.

**Notes on the distance fixes.**
- **Geometric-first (decision 2).** On GT boxes it halves the fused error (0.087 → 0.057; 30–60 m 0.092 → 0.050) and keeps the fused coverage (0.99 vs geometric 0.97). The remaining gap to geometric (0.040 on its own 3,579 objects) comes from the 40 extra boxes that only MiDaS covers (truncated, 0–10 m bin). On detected boxes geometric-first alone is only slightly better (0.128 → 0.122): there the old fusion partly cancelled the detector's long bias with MiDaS's short bias at range (30–60 m bias +0.006), which is luck, not a model.
- **Calibration (decision 1).** All three models improve every hold-out split; `offset_box` is best or tied in all three, best at 0–10 m (AEB range) in all three, and its box-height term is stable (0.070–0.076).
